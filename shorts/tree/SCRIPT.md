## Facts (sources)
- Jan Baptist van Helmont (1580-1644), Flemish physician/chemist, Brussels area (today's Belgium). Published
  posthumously in *Ortus Medicinae* (1648). (Wikipedia, "Jan Baptist van Helmont"; Hershey, *Plant Science Bulletin*.)
- He planted a 5 lb willow (≈2.3 kg) in 200 lb (≈90.7 kg) of oven-dried soil, covered the pot, and gave it only
  rain (or distilled) water for 5 years. The willow then weighed 169 lb 3 oz (≈76.7 kg); the re-dried soil had lost
  only about 2 oz (≈57 g). He concluded the tree was made of water. (same sources)
- What he missed: almost all of a plant's dry mass comes from CO2 taken from the air in photosynthesis, plus water;
  dry wood is about 50 % carbon by mass. (USDA Forest Service carbon accounting; IPCC default carbon fraction 0.47-0.5)
- Water climbs the trunk through xylem pulled by evaporation from the leaves (transpiration / cohesion-tension),
  no pump. Leaves take in CO2 through stomata (tiny pores). Sugar made in the leaves travels in the phloem and builds
  new wood; temperate trees add one growth ring per year. Deciduous trees drop their leaves in autumn. (USGS Water
  Science School, "Evapotranspiration"; any plant physiology text.)
